# Section 2: Talent Strategy

Actors are scared. Studios are scared of actors being scared. You can be the studio that talent wants to work with. This is a genuine competitive advantage in a market where everyone else is either exploiting performers or tiptoeing around them.

## The core offer to talent

**"We use AI to make you more valuable, not to replace you."**

Concretely:

- Your performance days are shorter and better supported (AI handles backgrounds, environments, VFX — you focus on acting)
- You get paid more per day because our budgets reallocate from crew/set costs to talent
- You own your digital twin data and can revoke consent at any time
- You get profit participation in AI uses of your performance (not a flat buyout)
- You see exactly how your likeness is used — full transparency, auditable pipeline
- Your work reaches more people through our multi-format expansion (your performance becomes a book, a comic, an international dub — and you participate in all of it)

## The talent advisory board

Before you approach any talent for projects, form a Talent Advisory Board of 3-5 performers who help shape your AI policies. This gives you:

- Credibility with agents and managers
- Real feedback on what performers actually worry about
- Names you can reference when pitching other talent
- Protection against "AI studio exploits actors" press narratives

**Who to approach for the board:**

- A mid-career character actor who's vocal about AI rights (they exist, they're on social media talking about this)
- A voice actor with SAG-AFTRA AI committee connections
- A director who's experimented with AI tools
- A showrunner who's curious but cautious

**Compensation:** paid advisory role, equity in the studio, first-look on projects

## How to get A-list talent attached (the real question)

A-list actors don't need you. They have offers stacked. Here's what might get them:

**Creative control they can't get elsewhere.** At a major studio, actors serve the IP. At your studio, offer them executive producer credit with real authority. "Come build a universe with us and own a piece of it" is a pitch no Marvel film makes.

**Economics that make sense.** Traditional studio: SAG minimum or negotiated rate, backend points that never pay out because of Hollywood accounting. Your studio: lower upfront (be honest about budgets), but real equity-like participation in the IP across all formats and the creator platform. When their character gets used by 10,000 creators, they earn from that.

**The cool factor.** Early-stage AI-native entertainment is genuinely interesting to a certain kind of actor — the ones who invested in tech companies, who have production companies, who want to build something. Your pitch to them is: "be the first A-list talent to build an AI universe and own it."

**The short-term path.** You won't get A-list for Project 1. Don't try. Sequence:

1. **Projects 1-3:** unknown but talented performers, offered generous terms. They become your studio's faces.
2. **Projects 4-5:** recognizable character actors. "I saw what you did with Project 1, that was interesting" — this is how the conversation starts.
3. **Project 6+ (year 2):** approach A-list talent with a proven pipeline, a library, a theater network, and a creator platform. Now the pitch has substance.

## What agents and managers care about

When you call CAA or WME, the agent's checklist is:

1. **Is this SAG signatory?** (Yes — from day one)
2. **What's the money?** (Be real about budgets, offer creative backend)
3. **How is AI being used with my client?** (Show them the consent and transparency infrastructure)
4. **Who else is attached?** (This is why the advisory board matters — names breed names)
5. **Is this going to be embarrassing?** (Show them finished work. Quality kills this objection.)

## Synthetic performers vs. human performers

Your studio will use both. The strategic position:

- **Hero characters:** always human-performed (even if AI-augmented). The audience connection to a real performer is your premium content signal.
- **Supporting and background:** mix of human and synthetic, clearly disclosed
- **Animation characters:** voices are human-performed, visuals are generated. This is the cleanest current model for AI entertainment.
- **Creator platform characters:** a library of original synthetic characters for platform users, with optional licensed real-performer characters (with consent and revenue share)

**Never try to pass a synthetic performer off as human.** The industry is moving toward mandatory disclosure, and being ahead of that curve is pure upside.

## The talent pipeline for a 500-acre studio

Long term, your land becomes a talent magnet:

- Residences or short-term housing for performers on extended shoots
- On-site training programs: "Learn to act for AI production" (the skills are slightly different — hitting marks for scans, performing for reference capture)
- Partnership with acting schools: your studio becomes a training ground
- Community theater program at your theater network locations: talent scouting from the grassroots
